## Summary

I designed and developed a visually captivating artist portfolio website that showcases the artist's creativity, features their artwork, provides basic museum information, and serves as a virtual gallery. My responsibilities included website design and layout, and I built it from scratch with HTML, CSS and JavaScript, without templates.

## Context and problem

Museo Carmona brings together the work of sculptor Edgardo Carmona. The website had three jobs: show his work, provide basic information about the museum and serve as a virtual gallery, with a visual identity that belonged to the artist.

The museum already had a website, but it looked disorganized and generic, and it didn't reflect the artist's aesthetic.

The artist wanted a space to publish all of his work and present it in different countries through art representatives. The site had to work as his introduction to that audience.

## Research

- **Conversations with the artist.** I listened to the vision he wanted to express and why he needed the site.
- **Analysis of the work.** I identified the colors in his pieces to build the site's palette from them, instead of imposing an outside aesthetic.
- **Site goal.** Bring all his work together in one place and present it to art representatives in different countries.

## Design process

- **Aesthetic and visual narrative.** I defined them together with the museum's owner, strengthening the artist's identity and digital presence.
- **Collection architecture.** I organized the full collection into a navigable digital gallery and structured the artist's information. The gallery is split by medium (sculptures, paintings, drawings, glass-ceramics, murals, vases, lamps and music), and sculptures are further split into abstract and figurative. Each piece shows its title and year.
- **Development.** I built the site from scratch with HTML, CSS and JavaScript, without templates: lightweight, responsive and functional.

![Home page with sculptor Edgardo Carmona next to one of his metal horse sculptures in front of the Eiffel Tower, and below it his name with a "Ver Galería" (View gallery) button](./02-inicio.png)

## Validation

The site was validated with the artist and refined over several iterations with him until it reached the published version.

## Final solution

A live site at [museocarmona.com](https://www.museocarmona.com/) (in Spanish) with three parts:

- **Home:** introduces the sculptor with a large-format image and leads straight to the gallery.
- **Virtual gallery:** the work organized by medium, with filters, the title and year of each piece, videos of some sculptures and a music player.
- **News:** works in progress and updates from the artist.

![News page with posts about works in progress: a colorful abstract painting and a sculpture of a dancer with a cello in the workshop](./03-noticias.png)

The site adapts to mobile screens with a collapsible menu.

![Mobile version of the home page, with the Carmona logo, the hamburger menu and the image of the sculptor](./04-movil.png)

## Learnings

- **Client relationship management.** I learned to communicate clearly, negotiate and reach agreements to build something as close as possible to what the artist imagined.
- **Balancing vision and technical constraints.** Not everything he imagined was feasible with the technology of the time. Part of the work was proposing alternatives and agreeing on solutions that respected his intent.
- **Turning a subjective vision into concrete design decisions.** Starting from the colors in his work and what he wanted to express gave an objective basis for discussing aesthetic choices with someone who has a very defined artistic identity.
- **Iterating with the client as part of the process.** Review rounds weren't a delay: each one brought the site closer to his vision and built trust in the result.
- **Designing for the end audience.** The site didn't just have to please the artist; it had to work for the art representatives who would see it from other countries.
